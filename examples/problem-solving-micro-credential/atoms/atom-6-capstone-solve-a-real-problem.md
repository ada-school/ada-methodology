## ⚛ Learning Atom 6 — *Capstone: Solve a Real Problem End-to-End*

**Phase:** 🐵 4 · Collaboration & Reflection (*share*) · **KSA:** 🛠️ Skill + 🌱 Ability
· **Target level:** L2 · **Component ids:** `S-frame`, `S-root-cause`, `S-solutions`,
`A-persistence`, `A-critical-curious`

### 🎯 Learning objective

- **Take a real, messy problem** from definition through root-cause to a **chosen, defended solution** —
  documenting the reasoning and **piloting** where possible — then **defend it** and peer-review.

### 🧩 Prerequisites

- All prior atoms.

### 🧭 Atom description

The capstone proves the whole process on something real. The learner picks an actual problem (work,
study, community, or a provided scenario), runs the full loop — define → diagnose → generate → decide →
(implement/pilot) → review — and presents not just the answer but the **reasoning**: how they framed
it, found the root cause, weighed options, and what they'd do next.

---

### 🚀 The brief

> Pick a **real, non-trivial problem** you can work on within the course window. Run the **full
> problem-solving process** and produce: a sharp **problem statement**, a **root-cause analysis**, a
> set of **options with a decision matrix**, your **chosen solution with the trade-off named**, and a
> **pilot or implementation plan** (with the success metric). Then **defend it** in a short session and
> peer-review a classmate.

Pick something **real and appropriately sized** — the grade is on the *quality of the thinking*, not
the size of the problem or whether the world is fully fixed by Friday.

---

### 📦 What to submit

- [ ] **Problem statement** — specific, measurable, neutral, solution-free (Atom 3), with the success
  metric.
- [ ] **Root-cause analysis** — 5 Whys and/or fishbone, plus how you **tested** the suspected cause
  (Atom 4).
- [ ] **Options + decision matrix** — ≥5 options, weighted criteria, the choice, and the **trade-off
  named** (Atom 5).
- [ ] **Pilot/implementation plan** — the smallest reversible test and what "it worked" looks like.
- [ ] **A reflection** (6–8 sentences) — where you got stuck, an assumption you had to discard, and a
  bias you caught yourself in.
- [ ] **A peer review you gave** — the capstone rubric applied to a classmate, with specific, useful
  feedback.

---

### 🎤 Showcase / defense structure (≈ 5 minutes)

1. **The problem** (1 min) — your statement and why it matters.
2. **The diagnosis** (1–2 min) — root cause + how you know (your test).
3. **The decision** (1–2 min) — options, the matrix, your choice, and the trade-off.
4. **Next & lessons** (30s) — pilot plan + one thing the process taught you.

Expect **challenge questions** — "what if your root cause is wrong?", "what option did you dismiss?" —
and answer them with your reasoning (that's the critical-curiosity Ability, observed live).

---

### ✅ Evaluate — Capstone (`capstone-5`)

Graded with the standard 5-criteria Assessment Rubric in [`../rubrics.md`](rubrics.md) /
[`../capstone.md`](capstone.md). This is the **third and decisive occasion** for the Abilities
`A-persistence` and `A-critical-curious`, alongside the Skills.

### 📦 Deliverable

- Your full case file (statement → root cause → options/matrix → choice → pilot plan) + reflection +
  the peer review you gave.

### 🧠 Final reflection

- Compare how you'd have "solved" this on day 1 vs. now. What changed in *how you think* about
  problems? Name one habit you'll keep.
